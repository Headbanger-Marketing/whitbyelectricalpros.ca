EV charger installation in Whitby.

An EV charger at a Whitby home relies on whatever supply can reach the parking spot, which is harder to judge at a detached garage or distant spot. Ask for any existing feed to be checked before a charger is chosen.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Whitby property

Sketch the route from the house to the parking spot in words, noting any fence, driveway surface, wall or finished ceiling the cable might cross. Photograph what is already mounted on the garage wall from the outside.

Ask the contractor whether any feed already reaches the garage, what it currently serves and whether it can be relied on for a charger or must be replaced by a new route.

What should the assessment establish?

A scope that names the charger and says nothing on the supply leaves you unable to tell what already reaches a distant Whitby parking spot, or what it serves now. Both belong in the scope, with whether that feed can be reused or a new route is needed. Who files the ESA notification is named, along with the cable route, the surfaces to be opened and the outdoor-rated parts. The contractor also explains how the proposed load relates to the rest of the home and whether the design includes a load-management arrangement.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the wiring already in my detached garage power a charger?

It depends on what the existing feed is, what it serves and how it was installed, which only an assessment can establish. Do not plug a charger into an existing outlet until the contractor has said it is suitable.

Does a longer distance to the parking spot change what is installed?

It can affect the cable route, the surfaces that must be crossed and the design the contractor recommends. Ask for the route to be described in writing so each quote covers the same distance and the same repairs.

Useful project details

  • Vehicle and charger model if chosen
  • Parking position and preferred equipment location
  • Indoor or outdoor conditions
  • Existing supply records if available
  • Other major loads and future charging needs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
