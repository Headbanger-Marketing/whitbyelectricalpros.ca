Renovation wiring in Whitby.

Renovation wiring in a Whitby home starts with deciding which existing circuits can be kept, and a secondary suite adds the question of what can serve a second household. Answer that before the electrical plans are fixed.

Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Whitby property

Gather the floor plans, note where each room of the suite will be and list the appliances it will contain. Photograph the existing panel labels and mark the ceilings between floors that are already finished.

Ask the contractor which existing circuits and cables, if any, can be kept for the suite, how new cable would pass between floors and what the municipality and ESA each need to be told.

What should the assessment establish?

A Whitby homeowner is creating a secondary suite. The contractor works out which parts of the present installation can serve a separate household and which must be new, naming each piece of retained wiring with the basis for keeping it. A layout shows how the suite's circuits are arranged, and a finding states whether the existing panel and supply were assessed for the added use. Where the house has two storeys and limited attic access, the scope traces how cable reaches the suite through finished surfaces. The building permission and the ESA notification appear as two separate steps.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the existing panel serve a secondary suite as well as the house?

That is a capacity question the contractor must assess, not something a spare breaker space proves. Ask for the finding in writing, including whether the supply itself was looked at.

Is an electrical notification the same as a building permit for a suite?

No. A building permit is a municipal matter, while the ESA notification covers the electrical work itself. A suite project may need both, and the contractor should say who files which.
