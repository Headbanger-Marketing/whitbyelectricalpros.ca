Before work begins

Check what is already there before you rely on it.

Does your home have two storeys and little or no usable attic? Then cable to the upper ceilings may have no simple path from above, and finished walls between floors can leave the contractor few ways through. Other layouts may limit routes differently.

Whatever the house, ask the contractor to confirm that a spare circuit, outlet, rough-in or panel space already in place is suitable before new equipment is planned around it. Note where each item is and who installed it.

How do I find out if an existing spare circuit can be used?

Gather any records, labels and photographs of the outside of the panel, then ask the contractor to assess the circuit. A spare position or a capped cable does not show that it suits the new equipment.

Can a rough-in left in a wall be relied on for a new load?

Not without an assessment. A rough-in shows that someone prepared a route or box, not what was intended or whether it was finished correctly. Ask the contractor to check it and put the finding in writing.

Why is cable routing harder in a two-storey house with a small attic?

Cable to an upper floor often has to pass through finished walls and ceilings, so the route affects the work involved. Ask the contractor to describe where cable would run and which surfaces may need repair afterwards.

What should I compare when quotes treat the existing wiring differently?

Compare what each quote assumes about the parts already installed. One may reuse an existing circuit while another replaces it, so ask each contractor to state what was checked and what was only assumed.
